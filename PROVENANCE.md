# Provenance and Evidence Policy

## Objective

ATTICUS Continuity should preserve not only conclusions but also where they came from, what supports them, and how confident we are.

## Minimum provenance record

For substantive knowledge, record where practical:

```yaml
id:
title:
date_recorded:
source:
source_type:
source_date:
claim:
evidence:
status:
confidence:
verification_method:
contradictions:
derived_from:
last_reviewed:
```

## Knowledge states

- `UNVERIFIED` — recorded but not independently checked.
- `SUPPORTED` — evidence materially supports the claim.
- `CONTESTED` — credible evidence or interpretation conflicts.
- `FALSIFIED` — evidence materially disproves the claim.
- `INFERRED` — reasoned conclusion extending beyond direct observation.
- `HYPOTHESIS` — testable proposition not yet established.
- `CANONICAL` — explicitly adopted governance or architectural state.
- `DEPRECATED` — retained for history but no longer controlling.

## Source hierarchy

Prefer, where available:

1. primary technical evidence, logs, source code, formal papers, official incident reports;
2. direct statements from responsible organizations or researchers;
3. high-quality independent analysis;
4. secondary summaries;
5. informal commentary.

Lower-tier evidence is not automatically false. It simply carries less evidentiary weight.

## Inference rule

Do not silently turn an inference into a fact.

Use explicit language such as:

- `Observation:`
- `Source claim:`
- `Inference:`
- `Hypothesis:`
- `Unknown:`

## Contradictions

Contradictory evidence should be retained rather than forced into premature reconciliation. Record the competing claims and what evidence would resolve them.

## AI-generated material

Model-generated analysis may be stored, but the model is not itself an authoritative source for external facts. Where a model synthesizes source material, retain citations or source references sufficient to reconstruct the derivation.

## Canonicalization

No material becomes canonical merely because it appears repeatedly, was generated by a capable model, or came from a prestigious institution.

Canonical status requires an explicit governance decision and should identify the superseded state when applicable.
