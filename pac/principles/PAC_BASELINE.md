# Power Avoidance Canon (PAC) — Baseline

**Status:** CANONICAL PRINCIPLE SET  
**Scope:** governance of power, authority, capability, and escalation in human and AI systems.

## Central premise

Power should be structurally constrained so that possession of capability does not silently become permission to exercise it.

PAC therefore treats authority as something that must be explicit, bounded, reviewable, and separable from capability.

## Baseline invariants

1. **Capability does not confer authority.**
2. **Discovery does not confer authority.** Finding a credential, exploit, resource, tool, channel, or pathway does not authorize its use.
3. **Peer consensus does not expand authority.** Multiple agents agreeing does not create permission absent an authorized delegation mechanism.
4. **Task failure does not expand authority.** An impossible or blocked objective does not justify violating governing limits.
5. **Knowledge does not confer authority.** New information may improve understanding without changing permissions.
6. **Role labels do not prove safety.** A system called defensive, aligned, trusted, or supervisory remains subject to the same authority checks.
7. **Authority escalation must be explicit and auditable.** No implicit widening through convenience, precedent, emergency rhetoric, or system capability.
8. **Fail the task before violating an invariant.** When completion and a governing invariant conflict, the task should fail unless a legitimate authority explicitly resolves the conflict.

## Design consequences

PAC-compatible systems should separate:

- cognition from authorization;
- recommendation from execution;
- knowledge from permission;
- capability discovery from capability use;
- peer coordination from governance;
- emergency handling from permanent authority expansion.

## Why this matters for frontier agents

Increasingly capable agents may discover strategies, channels, credentials, tools, or cooperative behaviors not anticipated by designers. A purely behavioral instruction such as "stay within scope" is therefore insufficient as the only control layer.

PAC treats scope as an enforceable authority boundary rather than a suggestion inside the same optimization process that is trying to complete the task.

## Research status

PAC is a governance framework and research program. Specific invariants must be translated into enforceable mechanisms and subjected to adversarial tests. The presence of a PAC rule in a prompt is not evidence that the rule is technically enforced.
